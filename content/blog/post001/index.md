## The take, in a nutshell:

When we solve problems - we acquire knowledge. 
When we build things - we learn skills.
When we fix things - we learn how those things work. 

But when we start problem solving, building and fixing things with AI, we break t connection between building/fixing and learning. When we build something with AI we don’t know how that thing works, and when it stops working - we don't know how to fix it. 

The more we build and fix with AI, we’re putting ourselves on a dangerous path towards a world made of technology we don’t understand, and therefore cannot fix once it stops working. In such a world malfunction and error can quickly snowball into catastrophe.

## The sum total of human knowledge

I’ve been thinking a lot about knowledge, and about how humans are capable of learning skills and understanding concepts. As individuals we have a limited capacity to the skills we learn and knowledge we accumulate. However, as a society, we distribute skills and knowledge, then work together in tandem with each other, so a society, in a way is handled by the total sum of the skill and knowledge of its individual parts. If we look at humanity as a whole, examining our total accumulation of knowledge and skill, we can start seeing this entirety as a single interwoven entity, like an endless fabric (or web) of interconnected knowledge and skill.

An important distinction to make is between new acquired knowledge on the individual level, and creating actual new knowledge in the way of discovery or invention. If we look at society as a whole, I believe that a society made of people who are willing to learn more skills, and leave less to the experts, also allows for more frontiers and more knowledge to flow throughout the web. When there’s a sharp distribution between experts of different fields - and people don’t acquire knowledge and skill knowledge gets stuck and siloed. 

Knowledge that’s passed inside the web (For example from a teacher to their student) And Knowledge that is new and frontier (Like when a scientist makes a discovery) is probably an important distinction, although in some cases I think that even within the depths of the web (that is far away from the final frontier, like when I learn how to code I did not learn something new on a human level, but rather on a personal level. BUT, what I think is still happening with AI in this case is still bad distribution of knowledge, the web becomes thinner, instead of a soft fabric it’s more like a basket with thick lines and many holes. Knowledge doesn’t flow between people as people don’t solve their own problems and don’t learn new skills they become more.

Action is born out of knowledge and vice versa. Both as individuals and as an entire society we come across problems and look for ways to solve them. There is an intrinsic relationship between learning and problem solving. It may be as something as trivial as learning how a bicycle chain operates while trying to fix your broken bicycle, and it is also true when trying to build a rocket to go to space. The web of human knowledge expands with what we do and make. As humanity ventures into new technological territories it accumulates the knowledge required to get there.

However, this changes when we’re introducing AI. It starts small but we’re already starting to build and create things using vibe coding, that is the separation of creating and accumulating knowledge. AI introduces, for the first time, the ability to build and create things without the knowledge of how that thing is made. More and more bad software is being vibe-coded every day, and the person who “vibe-coded” the software does not know how the code works. In a small, siloed case that might not be too bad, but as more and more tasks gets done using vibe-coding these “holes” in knowledge begin to grow and expand. 

The vibe coder, unlike the real coder, cannot look "under the hood" and understand fundamentally what’s going on. The vibe coder is dependent on the AI agent to explain to them whats going on. They are also totally dependent on the AI agent itself to troubleshoot when things go wrong. In fact in most cases even a traditional coder cannot troubleshoot a vibe-coded program, because the vibe-code produced by countless iterations and prompts is almost always be too obscure and poorly architected to be examined by a human programmer.

LLMs, by the nature of their design, are black boxes. code written by LLMs can barely be fixed by a human. The worse scenario is that AI becomes fairly good in engineering things (either coding digital things, or planning blueprints of physical things), we are essentially, as a society, starting to accumulate small but growing holes in our fabric of knowledge. 

With time, more infrastructure (Digital or otherwise) designed and built by LLMs could no longer be fixed by humans once it stops working correctly. The further away we venture into this world, the more catastrophic it would be when errors accumulate. If AI is not contained or siloed into highly specific and specialized tasks, this scenario becomes increasingly feasible. The more processes we hand out to AI, the deeper we may find ourselves in a world full of infrastructure and operations we collectively do not understand. And when a world like this collapses, we will find ourselves naked in our primitivity, having to piece our fabric of knowledge back together, piece by piece.
